How the other two are chosen

Every other pair of cells is tried, {{ candidates.toLocaleString('en-GB') }} candidates for each primary, as a secondary and a tertiary. The primary is the ground, the secondary partners it, and the tertiary is an accent laid on top, in roughly {{ split }} proportions. A candidate is dropped if any two of its colours sit closer than {{ minDistinct }} in OKLab, if the accent sits closer than {{ accentDistance }} to the ground, if no pair of its colours reaches {{ textContrast }}:1 contrast for text, or if it adds an olive. The survivors are ranked, and the best is shown.

The ranking adds two things which pull against each other. The first is harmony, from Ou and Luo's model of two-colour harmony, combined over the three pairs as in Ou, Chong, Luo and Minchew. It favours colours that share a hue and differ in lightness. The second is how well the accent stands out, since Schloss and Palmer found that while pairs are liked more the closer their hues, a figure on a ground is liked more the further its hue and lightness are from the ground's. It counts {{ figureWeight }} as much as harmony.

Three choices are this page's rather than the papers'. Pairs are weighted by how much of each colour there is, not equally. Ou and Luo's hue effect, which rates some hues as more harmonious than others whatever they are paired with, is left out of the ranking, except for its penalty on dark yellows; kept whole, it hands the same few pale yellows and blues to a large share of the wheel. And the weight above was set by looking at the aesthetic of every primary. The harmony figure shown is Ou and Luo's own, unmodified. Their observers were all Chinese students studying in the UK, and harmony is not the same for everyone.

How the wheel is built

The wheel is drawn in OKLCH, a colour space built so that equal lightness looks equally light, and its angle is the hue angle: a cell at 42° is hue 42°. There are {{ cells.length }} cells, {{ hueCount }} hues {{ hueStep }}° apart in {{ tones.length }} tones, from lightness {{ darkest }} at the centre to {{ lightest }} at the rim. Each cell is a single sRGB colour, as saturated as a screen can show at its lightness and hue, which is why a light yellow is vivid and a light blue is soft. The band outside the cells is the same colour function sampled every {{ continuumStep }}°, so the cells are visibly a choice of stops along it. Nothing you choose here is stored or sent.
